Plato the Wrestler: The Philosopher Who Burned His Plays

Did you know that before becoming one of history's most influential philosophers, Plato was an accomplished wrestler and even wrote tragedies? He reportedly abandoned his literary career by burning his plays after encountering Socrates.

Born Aristocles, Plato earned his famous nickname, meaning 'broad,' either due to his broad shoulders from wrestling or his broad intellectual scope. He competed in the Isthmian Games, a major panhellenic athletic festival. His decision to forsake playwriting for philosophy upon meeting Socrates highlights the profound impact of his mentor, leading him to establish the Academy, often considered the Western world's first institution of higher learning. This transformation underscores a foundational moment in his intellectual journey, redirecting his creative energy from drama to dialectic.
