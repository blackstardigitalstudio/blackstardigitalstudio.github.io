Glow-Worms: The Living Flashlights of WWI Trenches

Did you know that during World War I, soldiers in the trenches sometimes used jars filled with glow-worms as a safe, dim light source to read maps and orders without attracting enemy fire?

In the perpetual darkness and constant danger of the front lines, any artificial light source could instantly expose a soldier's position to enemy snipers or artillery. To circumvent this perilous issue, troops ingeniously collected female glow-worms (Lampyris noctiluca), whose abdomens emit a steady, cold bioluminescent light, placing them into jars. This living flashlight provided just enough dim illumination for soldiers to secretly read maps, orders, or letters, ensuring operational security while navigating the ever-present dangers of trench warfare.
