Why Did Ancient Greek Olympians Compete in the Nude?

Ancient Greek Olympic athletes famously competed entirely naked, a practice called 'gymnos' (from which 'gymnasium' derives), which emphasized the beauty of the human form and reverence for the gods.

This tradition is believed to have started around the 8th century BCE. While initially athletes wore loincloths, a famous story recounts a runner losing his in a race and winning, prompting the general adoption of nudity. This practice also served to distinguish free Greek citizens from slaves and non-Greeks, showcasing the physical perfection cultivated through their rigorous training. Furthermore, given the Games were dedicated to Zeus, nudity was seen as a form of reverence, presenting the athletes as pure and unencumbered before the gods.
