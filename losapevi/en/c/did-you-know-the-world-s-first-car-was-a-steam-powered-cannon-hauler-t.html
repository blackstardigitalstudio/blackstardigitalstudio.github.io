Did You Know? The World's First 'Car' Was a Steam-Powered Cannon Hauler That Crashed!

In 1769, Nicolas-Joseph Cugnot created the world's first self-propelled mechanical land vehicle, a steam-powered tricycle designed to haul artillery. It was so unwieldy and difficult to steer that it famously crashed into a wall during one of its early trials, marking history's first recorded automobile accident.

Cugnot's "Fardier à vapeur" (steam dray) was an experimental military tractor built for the French Army. Weighing over 2.5 tons and powered by a large, front-mounted copper boiler, it could only manage speeds around 2.25 mph and needed to stop frequently to rebuild steam pressure. The vehicle's poor weight distribution and lack of effective brakes contributed to its notoriously difficult handling, leading to the 1771 collision with a stone wall that ended its operational career. Though impractical, it laid crucial groundwork for future automotive engineering.
