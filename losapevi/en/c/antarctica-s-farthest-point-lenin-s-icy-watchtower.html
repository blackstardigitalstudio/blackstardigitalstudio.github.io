Antarctica's Farthest Point: Lenin's Icy Watchtower

Did you know? The Southern Pole of Inaccessibility (SPOI) in Antarctica, the continent's farthest point from any ocean, hosts an abandoned Soviet research station. A bust of Vladimir Lenin, which points towards Moscow, still protrudes from the snow and ice at this incredibly remote site.

The Soviet Union established the Pole of Inaccessibility Station in December 1958 during the International Geophysical Year. Intended for scientific research in extreme conditions, the station was only operational for 12 days before being evacuated due to the immense challenges of sustaining it. Before their departure, the expedition team left behind a plastic bust of Lenin atop a small building, marking their achievement at one of Earth's most remote locations. Today, the station is buried under meters of snow, but Lenin's bust continues to defy the elements, serving as an anachronistic historical marker.
