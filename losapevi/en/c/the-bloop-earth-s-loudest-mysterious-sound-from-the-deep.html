The Bloop: Earth's Loudest Mysterious Sound From the Deep?

In 1997, NOAA hydrophones detected an ultra-low-frequency, high-amplitude underwater sound in the Pacific, louder than any known animal, leading to years of speculation about its unknown origin.

"The Bloop" was detected repeatedly by hydrophones of the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) in 1997, spanning over a minute from a source in the remote South Pacific. Its ultra-low frequency and immense volume—far exceeding that of any known biological creature—initially baffled scientists, as it didn't match the signature of whales, volcanoes, or man-made sounds. For years, its true origin remained an intriguing mystery, sparking theories about colossal unknown deep-sea organisms. While NOAA later attributed it to icequakes caused by large icebergs fracturing, the initial detection of "The Bloop" highlighted the vast, unexplored mysteries of our oceans.
