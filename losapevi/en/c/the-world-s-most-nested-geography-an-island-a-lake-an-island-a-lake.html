The World's Most Nested Geography: An Island, a Lake, an Island, a Lake...

Did you know? The Philippines is home to one of the most geographically recursive places on Earth: an island (Vulcan Point) inside a lake (Main Crater Lake) which is on an island (Volcano Island) within a larger lake (Taal Lake), all located on the island of Luzon.

Vulcan Point is a small, rocky island, just meters across, formed from part of the Taal Volcano's central cone. This unique geological formation is a result of the complex volcanic activity of Taal, one of the most active volcanoes in the Philippines. The Main Crater Lake fills the caldera formed by previous eruptions on Volcano Island, which itself rises from the vast Taal Lake, a freshwater body occupying an even older volcanic caldera. This multi-layered natural wonder offers a stunning example of nested geological features.
