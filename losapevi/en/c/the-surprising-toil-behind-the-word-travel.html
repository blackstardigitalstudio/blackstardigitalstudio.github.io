The Surprising Toil Behind the Word 'Travel'

Did you know the English word 'travel' originates from the Old French 'travail,' meaning 'work' or 'toil'? For centuries, long journeys were arduous endeavors, not leisurely escapes.

Before the advent of modern transportation, undertaking a journey across significant distances was often a difficult and dangerous undertaking, fraught with physical exertion, exposure to elements, and the risk of robbery or illness. The etymology perfectly captures this reality, linking the very act of movement with suffering and hard labor. It wasn't until the development of railways, steamships, and later air travel that the perception of 'travel' shifted dramatically from a necessity of toil to an opportunity for leisure and exploration.
