Did Ancient Greeks Invent the World's First Computer?

Discovered in a shipwreck, the 2,000-year-old Antikythera Mechanism is widely considered the world's first analog computer, baffling experts with its advanced engineering.

This intricate bronze device, composed of dozens of meshing gears, was designed to predict astronomical positions, eclipses, and even Olympic game cycles with astounding accuracy. Its complexity, including differential gearing, represents a level of mechanical sophistication not seen again for over a millennium until medieval clockworks. The existence of such a machine rewrites the history of technology, suggesting a lost era of incredible ancient Greek engineering prowess.
