The Taos Hum: Is the Earth Humming a Secret Tune?

For decades, a small percentage of residents in Taos, New Mexico, have reported hearing a persistent, low-frequency hum that no scientific instrument has ever definitively located or identified, leaving its source a persistent mystery. While some describe it as a faint rumble, others liken it to a distant diesel engine, but it remains elusive to most visitors and scientific probes.

The Taos Hum, first widely reported in the early 1990s, is a phenomenon where only about 2% of the local population can perceive a continuous, low-pitched sound. Extensive studies have been conducted, including those by the University of New Mexico and the Los Alamos National Laboratory, but have failed to pinpoint an external acoustic source, suggesting it might be a psychosomatic phenomenon, a geological anomaly, or even related to electromagnetic radiation. Its subjective nature—only some people hear it, and it often disappears when they leave Taos—adds to its perplexing character, making it one of the world's most enduring auditory enigmas.
