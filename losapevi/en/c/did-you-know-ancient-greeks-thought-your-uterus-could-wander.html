DID YOU KNOW…

Did you know? Ancient Greeks thought your uterus could wander!

Did you know that ancient Greek physicians once attributed a wide range of female ailments, from anxiety to suffocation, to a 'wandering uterus' that could freely roam inside the body?

This fascinating but now debunked theory, rooted in the Greek word *hysterika* (uterus), proposed that the uterus, if unmoored or seeking moisture, might travel to different organs, causing symptoms depending on its location. Ancient physicians, influenced by Hippocratic writings, believed that if a woman did not marry or have children, her uterus would grow restless and move freely throughout the body. Treatments varied wildly, from applying foul-smelling substances to the head to drive it down, to sweet fragrances to lure it back into place. They even prescribed marriage and pregnancy to 'anchor' the organ, believing sexual activity and procreation would keep it in its proper position. The concept of hysteria, stemming directly from this 'wandering womb' idea, persisted in medical texts for nearly two millennia, shaping understandings of women's health until scientific advancements eventually disproved it.
