Lilith: From Ancient Sumerian Demoness to Adam's Rebellious First Wife?

While most famously known in Jewish folklore as Adam's defiant first wife, the mythical figure of Lilith might have roots stretching back thousands of years to ancient Sumerian and Babylonian mythology, where she appeared as a class of dangerous demonic entities called *lilitu*.

In ancient Sumerian and Babylonian mythology, the *lilitu* were believed to be malevolent wind spirits or night demons, often associated with owls and considered particularly dangerous to pregnant women and infants. These ancient Semitic demons, whose presence is recorded in texts as early as the Sumerian King List and later in the Babylonian Talmud, are thought to be the precursors to the singular, iconic figure of Lilith. Her subsequent development in Jewish mysticism, especially in the medieval text *Alphabet of Ben Sira*, transformed her from a generic demoness into a specific, defiant female who refused to be subservient to Adam, showcasing the fascinating evolution of mythological figures across cultures and millennia.
