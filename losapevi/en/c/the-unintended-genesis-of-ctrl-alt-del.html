The Unintended Genesis of Ctrl+Alt+Del

The ubiquitous "Ctrl+Alt+Del" keyboard command, now essential for troubleshooting, was originally conceived by IBM engineer David Bradley as a simple, unrecoverable developer-only shortcut to reboot a locked-up IBM PC during its early development.

Bradley intentionally chose the combination for its difficulty to press accidentally with one hand, requiring two hands to execute. He never intended it for end-users, viewing it purely as a debugging tool for software engineers. However, as the IBM PC became widespread, this "three-finger salute" unexpectedly transcended its original purpose, becoming the most famous keyboard shortcut for countless users needing to escape a frozen system, solidifying its place in computing history.
