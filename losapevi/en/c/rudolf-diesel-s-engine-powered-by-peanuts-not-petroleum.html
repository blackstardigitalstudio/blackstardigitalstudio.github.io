Rudolf Diesel's Engine: Powered by Peanuts, Not Petroleum!

Rudolf Diesel's pioneering engine, unveiled in 1897, wasn't initially designed for petroleum diesel; it famously ran on a variety of fuels, including peanut oil, showcasing a surprising early focus on versatile and renewable energy sources.

Diesel envisioned his invention as a flexible power source for diverse applications, aiming for independence from specific fuel types. At the 1900 World's Fair in Paris, an engine powered by peanut oil demonstrated this adaptability, operating for 200 hours. This foresight, born from a desire for high efficiency and cheaper, locally available fuels, reveals a surprising historical parallel to modern renewable energy efforts, long before petroleum diesel became the dominant fuel.
