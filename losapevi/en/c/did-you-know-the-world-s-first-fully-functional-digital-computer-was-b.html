Did you know? The World's First Fully Functional Digital Computer Was Built in 1941?

Long before the ENIAC, the world's first functional, programmable, fully automatic digital computer, the Z3, was completed in 1941 by Konrad Zuse in Germany, years earlier than many might expect.

Constructed during World War II, the Z3 was an electro-mechanical marvel, utilizing thousands of relays to perform floating-point binary arithmetic and could execute complex sequences of calculations. Despite its groundbreaking design, Zuse's work received limited recognition outside Germany at the time due to the war. The original machine was tragically destroyed in a 1943 bombing raid, but a modern replica, crucial for understanding early computer history, is displayed at the Deutsches Museum in Munich. This pioneering machine showcased the ingenuity of pre-silicon computing and laid foundational concepts for future digital computers.
